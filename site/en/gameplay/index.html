Gameplay: how the mod plays

Version 4.2 changes how a match plays, if you want it to: difficulty, dribbling, set pieces and position traits, each with a choice of its own. And one switch gives you back the 1996 game, exactly.

Where: OPTIONS → GAMEPLAY · DIFFICULTY: EASY, NORMAL, HARD · DRIBBLING: CLASSIC, NEW, CUSTOM · SET PIECES: CLASSIC, REALISTIC, ARCADE · ROLES: position traits · Cards and injuries · Set-piece practice

Where: OPTIONS → GAMEPLAY

Everything is in one row of the game's OPTIONS menu: GAMEPLAY. It has two values:

  • ORIGINAL: the game as it was in 1996, exactly. Every option on this page acts as in the original game, but what you chose is not forgotten: go back to CUSTOM and your settings are there as you left them.
  • CUSTOM (the default): press FIRE on it and the CUSTOM GAMEPLAY page opens, one row per option.

On that page LEFT / RIGHT change a row's value and FIRE moves it on to the next one. DEFAULTS puts every row back to its start value, BACK leaves the page. A career remembers its choices: a career saved on ORIGINAL stays on ORIGINAL.

The new options all start from the original game (NORMAL, CLASSIC, CLASSIC, ROLES OFF), so nothing changes until you choose. If you played 4.1, GAMEPLAY starts on CUSTOM with the settings you already had.

DIFFICULTY: EASY, NORMAL, HARD

How strong the computer is when it plays against you. NORMAL is the original game.

  • EASY: the computer team's players are 80% as good, its goalkeeper is 2 points weaker and its passes go astray more often.
  • HARD: its players are 10% better, its goalkeeper 2 points stronger and its passes more accurate.

The level changes only a computer team that plays against you. Two human players, computer against computer, training games, SWOS++ (the add-on for the English game) and simulated results (the matches the game works out without playing them) always play the original game.

DRIBBLING: CLASSIC, NEW, CUSTOM

Dribbling means running past an opponent with the ball at your feet. CLASSIC is the original game; NEW is the mod's new dribbling, ready to play; CUSTOM lets you set each part of it yourself. With NEW:

  • your player slows down less when he runs with the ball;
  • on sharp turns the ball stays closer to his feet;
  • after winning the ball you keep it a moment longer before anyone can take it back;
  • the computer slides in less often, and never while you dribble inside its own penalty area;
  • its second man holds back further, instead of closing in on you together with the first;
  • after losing the ball to a strong tackle, the computer needs a moment longer to win it back.

To be honest: in 4.2 the computer's defenders press and cover more (fewer 'skittles' standing still while you go past them), but they don't tackle more often.

These settings apply only while the ball is in play: free kicks, corners, throw-ins, goal kicks and kick-offs stay as in the original game. Like DIFFICULTY, they act in your matches against the computer.

The CUSTOM DRIBBLING page

DRIBBLING on CUSTOM opens a page of its own. A number row shows a step: 0 is the original game, and the higher the step, the more help you get (the legend at the bottom: LEFT: CLASSIC, RIGHT: MORE HELP). DEFAULTS puts back the values of NEW, for example SPEED WITH BALL 2 of 4 and BALL CLOSE ON TURNS 4 of 10; BACK returns to the CUSTOM GAMEPLAY page.

  • SPEED WITH BALL: how little your player slows down when he runs with the ball.
  • BALL CLOSE ON TURNS: how close the ball stays to his feet on sharp turns.
  • BALL SHIELDING: how long you keep the ball after winning it, before an opponent can take it back.
  • FEWER CPU SLIDES: the computer's players start a sliding tackle only from closer, so less often.
  • LESS CPU DOUBLING: how far back the computer's second man stays, instead of closing in on you with the first.
  • CPU WIN BACK DELAY: how long the computer waits before going for the ball again after losing it to a strong tackle.
  • NO SLIDES IN BOX: ON, the computer never slides in while you dribble inside its penalty area, where every foul is a penalty; OFF, it can, as in the original game. ON with NEW.
  • CPU DEFENDERS: how the computer's defenders face you. CHASE: they run after you, as in the original game (the value of NEW); WAIT: they stay between you and the goal instead of chasing you from behind; COVER and ONE AT A TIME: one man presses you and a team-mate covers behind him.
  • CPU BUILD UP: ON, the computer's team builds its play more calmly: its goalkeeper looks for a free team-mate more often and, under pressure, it kicks the ball long less often. OFF, as in the original game. ON with NEW.

SET PIECES: CLASSIC, REALISTIC, ARCADE

Set pieces are the restarts with the ball standing still: free kicks, corners and the like. Three values, for every match; CLASSIC is the original game.

REALISTIC:

  • headers from corners and crosses go down instead of over the bar; a good header of the ball keeps it under the bar from further out;
  • goalkeepers no longer catch everything that drops into the six-yard box;
  • the computer goes for high balls more often;
  • free kicks near the box dip over the wall the way real ones do, and how often they go in depends on the taker's shooting, as in real football: a top specialist scores about one in ten, an average taker one in fifteen, a weak one about one in thirty. When the computer's free kick misses, it goes over the bar or into the wall.

ARCADE:

  • the same headers and goalkeepers as REALISTIC;
  • more free kicks around the box: there, a clean tackle is whistled as a foul one time in three;
  • the computer curls its free kicks towards the far post: a top shooter scores about one in six from 20-25 metres, a weak one hardly ever.

Your own free kicks stay in your hands, direction and curl, in every mode.

ROLES: position traits

A position trait is something a player does better because of his position. With ROLES on ON (it starts on OFF, the original game) every team has them, the computer's too. The position is the player's natural one, the one in his team's file, not the place where you put him in the line-up. An idea of Marco's.

  • Headers down in the opponents' box: any outfield player heading the ball inside the opponents' penalty area aims it down, the more so the better he is in the air. Lobbed headers (stick held back) and standing headers don't change; anywhere else, headers go up and away as in the original game.
  • The winger's whipped cross: a right or left winger (RW / LW) out wide in the last quarter of the pitch plays a low, fast cross into the box with the 'back' after-touch (the stick moved just after the kick) instead of the high ball. The same from a corner or a free kick out wide taken by a winger, when the ball goes towards the middle. The computer's wingers rarely play this ball, so the whipped cross is mostly yours.
  • Centre-backs (D) reach high balls a little further in their own penalty area, the more so the better they are in the air.

With SET PIECES on REALISTIC or ARCADE every header already goes down; with ROLES on, only those in the opponents' box do. ROLES OFF, GAMEPLAY ORIGINAL, SWOS++ and training games always play the original game; the set-piece practice uses the traits as a match would.

Cards and injuries

The CUSTOM GAMEPLAY page also holds three options from earlier versions of the mod, ON at the start:

  • CPU RED CARDS: the computer's players are booked and sent off like yours. In the original game a computer player was never sent off and never got a second yellow card.
  • CPU INJURIES: the computer's players get injured like yours.
  • GOALKEEPER INJURIES: after every match each team's goalkeeper has a 1 in 25 chance of an injury that keeps him out for one or two matches.

OFF, or GAMEPLAY on ORIGINAL, plays the original game.

Set-piece practice

In a career, the TRAINING button on your squad screen now opens a page of its own:

  • TRAINING MATCH: the training game, as before;
  • FREE KICK PRACTICE, PENALTY PRACTICE, CORNER PRACTICE: the set piece comes again and again, the ball placed for you each time: free kicks from the centre and from both sides at four distances from goal, penalties, corners from both sides. No match clock, no stadium presentation, no score bar: just the set pieces;
  • PLAY IN ATTACK / PLAY IN DEFENCE: in attack your team takes the set piece; in defence the computer takes it and you defend.

SHOTS sets the number of attempts: 5, 10, 20, 50 or UNLIMITED (10 at first). HALF sets the goal: RANDOM (the one the match gives you), TOP, BOTTOM or ALTERNATING. Press ESC whenever you like. A results page then shows SHOTS and GOALS: OK takes you back, TRY AGAIN starts another series of the same practice.

A practice never changes your career more than a training game already does. With GAMEPLAY on ORIGINAL the page title reads TRAINING - GAMEPLAY: ORIGINAL and the practice plays the classic set pieces.
