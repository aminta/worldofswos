Playing styles for CPU teams

An idea we are starting to work on for the SWOS 96/97 Mod: career opponents that don't all play the same way. Some hit long balls, some build from the back, some press you high, some sit deep and counter. This page explains the idea, what the original game already has under the hood, and what we'd like your opinion on.

work in progress nothing final, no release date feedback welcome

Where we are today

A test version of the mod adds two new rows to the OPTIONS menu. Both only change matches you play against the computer; matches between two humans and simulated results stay exactly as in the original game.

OptionValuesWhat it does
DIFFICULTYEASY / NORMAL / HARDHow strong the CPU team is: its players' skills (80% / 100% / 110%), its keeper, how often it misplaces a pass. NORMAL is the original game.
DRIBBLINGCLASSIC / NEWNEW gives your ball carrier a bit more pace and control, protects you a little longer after you win the ball, and stops the CPU sliding in on you inside its own box (in the original, every foul there is a penalty). The CPU also plays a bit more on the ground: its keeper rolls it out to a free defender and its midfielders try some short passes instead of always clearing. CLASSIC is the original game.

Davide's first playtest: dribbling past two or three men feels good again, no more penalty after every run into the box, and a few honest fouls on the edge of the area.

The idea: a style for every club

In the original game every CPU team takes its decisions the same way. The only difference between clubs is their players' skills and their tactic. A playing style would change the decisions themselves: when to clear and when to pass, how hard to press, when to slide in, where to shoot from.

  • A style changes how a team plays, never how strong it is. Strength stays with DIFFICULTY and the players' skills. We will check that no style wins more on its own.
  • Your own control of the ball does not depend on the opponent's style. DRIBBLING stays a separate option.
  • A club can mix up to three styles, for example mostly build-up with some pressing. The mix is fixed for the whole match, so a team has a recognisable character.

The proposed styles

Neutral

today's DRIBBLING NEW opponent

You face: a keeper who plays short when a defender is free, fewer hopeful clearances, the odd short pass in midfield

Long ball

the classic SWOS CPU, a bit more so

You face: high balls over your midfield, second balls, lots of headers

Build-up from the back

needs a new "pass sideways and back" brain

You face: keeper to defender, patient passing through midfield; press them high and you can win the ball near their goal

High press

You face: no time on the ball in your own half, two men closing you down, more slide tackles

Defend and counter

You face: space in midfield, a wall in front of the box, quick forward passes the moment you lose the ball

Physical

You face: harder and earlier tackles, more fouls and cards (the referee's rules stay the same)

Long shots

You face: shots from 25–30 metres and a busy keeper; closing down the man on the ball matters

Styles and SWOS tactics

The game's tactics (4-4-2, 5-4-1, 4-2-4 and your own) are a map of positions: for each area of the pitch where the ball is, they say where each outfield player stands. They shape the team, but they never decide what the player on the ball does.

Styles are those decisions. The two work together: a build-up team finds more free men in a shape that offers them, a long-ball team suits two strikers up front. Every club keeps the tactic it has today.

Which club plays which style

Set by us

  • Well-known clubs: chosen from how they really play, checked on sources, as we do for the rest of the mod's data.
  • Everyone else: a rule by country and division (lower leagues lean towards long ball and physical), with some variety so a league isn't twenty copies.
  • Per era: the same club can play differently in the 1984-85, 1996-97 and 2026-27 worlds.

Changed by you

  • Fans often know best how their club plays. The plan is to let players reassign styles, for example in the web editor.
  • Your choices would live in a file of your own that mod updates never overwrite.
  • Good suggestions can also go into the mod's own list for everyone.

What the original engine already has

Everything above is built on numbers that already exist in SWOS 96/97's match engine. Each is a fixed value, the same for every team; a style would only change some of them for the CPU side. A few examples:

AreaIn the original game
Ball carrierRuns at 87.5% of his speed. How far the ball rolls ahead of him and how many turns he can make before losing it both depend on ball control (C).
Winning the ballAfter a successful tackle, nobody can take the ball back for a short moment (the same short lock for everyone).
Tackles and foulsAlmost every foul comes from a slide tackle that is still fast when it hits, made from behind or not clean. Every foul in the box is a penalty.
RefereeOne strictness value is drawn at kick-off and depends on match length. About 1 card in 8 is a straight red; a last-man foul is always a card, usually red.
InjuriesA chance on every slide contact, lower in longer matches, doubled for a player who is already injured.
CPU with the ballUnpressed, it never passes and runs at goal. Pressed in its own half, it clears long straight away. The keeper picks short or long with a coin flip.
CPU shootingShoots from inside a fixed distance, only sometimes from the edge of that range.
KeepersEach skill level has its own row of movement, reach, parry and catch values.
StaminaThere is none: players never tire during a match.

Your opinion

  • Do these six styles cover the football you'd like to face? Is one missing, or one you would drop?
  • Should the opponent's style be shown before the match (e.g. on the line-ups screen), or should you find out by playing?
  • Mixing: is "up to three styles per club" enough, or too much?
  • Would you rather the CPU change style during a match (for example pressing when losing late on)?
  • Which clubs, from any era, do you know well enough to suggest their style?

First step: a test version with two opposite styles (long ball and high press) forced on every opponent, to check that the difference is clearly felt before we assign styles to thousands of clubs.
