Les nouveautés de la 4.1

Télécharger le patcher

4.1 is a big release. It brings a world selector on the main menu (2026-27, 1996-97 or Europe 1984-85, for career, friendlies and DIY competitions), two players on one keyboard, the computer's clubs playing by your rules (red cards, injuries, and goalkeeper injuries for everyone), club finances closer to real football and a financial fair play option, B-team call-ups for 20 clubs, the continental cups of the other continents in the game's own screens, two-row benches you can see in the match with the opponent's squad one key away, stoppage time, the UEFA Super Cup and the other intercontinental matches, full stadium names, a bench info strip, more realistic offers for your players, white hair for Ravanelli, an easier install (a new patcher button and clearer messages) and a good number of fixes. Everything in 4.0 to 4.0.5 stays as it was; careers saved with those versions load and play on.

Nouveautés

The 4.0.5 save bug (thanks to Francesco and Tally)

What happened in 4.0.5. Playing two or more matches for real (not simulated) without quitting the game could damage your career save, so that it no longer loads (a "data/team..." error and a black screen), or freeze the game at the end of a season. It affected all languages, CD and GOG. 4.0.4 and earlier versions were not affected.

What 4.1 does. 4.1 fixes the cause, so you can play as many matches as you like in one go. It also repairs damaged World 2026-27 and Europe 1984-85 careers when you load them: keep the damaged save and just load it in 4.1. A 1996-97 career damaged in 4.0.5 cannot be repaired: load an earlier save.

Thanks to Francesco and Tally, who sent us their saves.

Two players on one keyboard

  • CONTROLS: new device "P1 AND P2 ON KEYBOARD". Two people can now play on the same keyboard. Player 2 has a second set of keys, by default W / S / A / D to move and left SHIFT to fire; you can change them in OPTIONS > CONTROLS like the first set, and they are saved in a file of their own (CONTROL2.DAT). Player 1 keeps his usual keys.
  • Good to know: many keyboards cannot read too many keys pressed at once (this is called ghosting: the keyboard "forgets" a key when several others are held down). If a direction or the fire key does not answer when both players press a lot at once, choose other keys for player 2 that are far from player 1's.
  • With SWOS++ this device is not offered (SWOS++ has its own controls).
  • The keyboard is never blocked by the joypad. When the controls are set to a joypad for player 1 and the keyboard for player 2 (the default when the patcher's "I have a joypad" is ticked), a joypad that is not centred - not calibrated, worn, or reading a corner - used to win over the keyboard: in the menus a direction held on the keyboard could do nothing (for example DOWN, with a stick leaning up). Now a direction held on the keyboard always counts, in the menus and for your team in a match, as long as nobody else is playing on the keyboard in that match. With KEYBOARD ONLY, TWO JOYPADS or SWOS++ nothing changes.

The computer's clubs play by the same rules as you

  • Two new OPTIONS rows, CPU RED CARDS and CPU INJURIES, both ON by default: the computer's clubs now get red cards (a sent-off player is suspended, like yours) and injuries (an injured player of a computer's club misses the following matches instead of being fit again at once). Turn a row OFF to get the original game behaviour back.
  • Under SWOS++ the two rows are hidden and the original behaviour applies.
  • Goalkeepers get injured too. In the original game a goalkeeper almost never gets injured. Now, after every match, each team's goalkeeper has a 1 in 25 chance of an injury. You see it afterwards, on the team screen, not during the match. He misses the next match or the next two, then he is fit again like any injured player. It is the goalkeeper on the pitch at the final whistle, and matches you only view the result of count too. The computer's goalkeepers get injured in every competition except friendlies, while CPU INJURIES is ON. In a DIY or preset competition the computer's injured and suspended players now get better after each match, like yours (before, they stayed out for the whole competition). To switch it off: OPTIONS > GOALKEEPER INJURIES (IT INFORTUNI PORTIERI, FR BLESSURES DES GARDIENS, DE TORWARTVERLETZUNGEN). It is ON by default and saved with the career; an older career loads it ON. Not with SWOS++.

Continental cups in the game's own screens (World 2026-27)

  • CONTINENTAL CUPS (VIEW COMPETITIONS): pick a confederation and you get a menu of its rounds already drawn, in the order they are played. Each round opens in the game's own cup screen, exactly as far as your career has got:
    • knockout rounds with both legs, the aggregate, extra time, away goals and penalties;
    • group stages with their tables and the fixtures match day by match day (NEXT shows the next stage, as in any cup of the game).
    • SUMMARY (IT RIEPILOGO, FR RESUME, DE ÜBERSICHT) is now inside STAT, in every cup (the UEFA ones too), with the points beside the clubs. For your own club's European cup (Champions League, Europa League or Conference League) it keeps the story of the season, and saves it with the career:
      • Earlier rounds. The summary lists the knockout rounds already played, from the play-offs up to the semi-finals, with the clubs that went through, before the round you are looking at. They are remembered in the save, so they are still there after you quit and load the career again.
      • LEAGUE PHASE. Once the knockout rounds start, a LEAGUE PHASE block (IT FASE CAMPIONATO, FR PHASE DE LIGUE, DE LIGAPHASE) shows the final table of the 36-club league phase, place by place with each club's points, before the PLAY-OFFS (IT SPAREGGI, FR BARRAGES, DE PLAYOFFS).
      • Rules. Only your own club's European cup is remembered (the other European cups of the world are shown as they stand at the moment). A new season starts the record again, and so does a new career; a club that skips the play-offs does not show last season's ties. A career saved before 4.1 shows the summary as it stands, without the earlier rounds.
      • A menu of green boxes. STAT > SUMMARY of your European cup opens a menu with one green box per phase played so far (league phase, play-offs, round of 16, quarter-finals, semi-finals, final). Each box opens that phase in the game's own screens, and the finished league phase shows as a table of places and points. The SUMMARY button at the bottom of the menu is the full text page.
  • VIEW WORLD: every continent now shows its continental cups as green buttons above its nations - SOUTH AMERICA the Libertadores, the Sudamericana and the Recopa, EUROPE the Champions League, Europa League and Conference League, WORLD the Intercontinental, and so on. A button opens that cup directly on its current round. The menu of rounds stays only on the CONTINENTAL CUPS path, where you pick a confederation.
  • When the game's own screen can't show a round, you get the text page instead:
    • for the AFC Champions League Elite league stage, a format the game's screens don't have;
    • in the rare cases where it couldn't be shown safely (too many clubs loaded at once, a club missing from the data).
  • Looking at the cups never changes your career or its save: everything is put back exactly as it was when you leave the screen.
  • If your club is playing a continental group stage, its round shows the real group you are playing in.
  • Continental cups, first season: in a new World 2026-27 career, CONTINENTAL CUPS now shows each cup's drawn round from the very first week: open it to see the groups or ties still to be played. The Intercontinental Cup, whose clubs are the first season's winners, lists its rounds still to be played.
  • More realistic scores in the continental cups: about 2.6 goals a match instead of more than 5. A career saved with an earlier version finishes its current continental season with the old scores; from the next season on it uses the new ones.

Super Cups and intercontinental cups (World 2026-27)

  • UEFA Super Cup.
    • It is played in mid-August (week of 12 August), before the national Super Cup: the Champions League winner against the Europa League winner of the season just ended.
    • One match on neutral ground. If it is level after 90 minutes, it goes straight to penalties (no extra time, as in the real one since 2025).
    • If the same club won both cups, its opponent is the Conference League winner. If there is no other winner, the match is not played.
    • It starts from the second season: in the world's first season the real 2026 edition has already been played.
    • You play it when your club is one of the two. It is in the calendar and in the season record.
    • Its rules are on the Champions League's FORMAT page.
  • Recopa Sudamericana moves to February, as the real one. Two legs, with the Libertadores champion at home in the second. If the tie is level on aggregate: extra time, then penalties (2026 edition: Lanús won in Rio after extra time).
  • CAF Super Cup moves to October, as the 2025 edition. One match on neutral ground, straight to penalties.
  • FIFA Intercontinental Cup. The Derby of the Americas (the Libertadores winner against the CONCACAF champion) moves to December, as the real one, followed by the Challenger Cup and the final. The play-off stays at the end of August and the Africa-Asia-Pacific Cup in September.

Stoppage time

  • Stoppage time (an idea of Davide and Marco): every half now has real added time. At 45:00 and 90:00 the clock stops and a "+N" appears next to it, like the fourth official's board: N added minutes, more when the half had goals, substitutions and cards (in the World 2026-27 career also the time lost with the ball out of play, as in today's football) - at most 3 in the 1996-97 game (2 in the first half), 5 in the World 2026-27 career (3 in the first half), 2 in the Europe 1984-85 career (1 in the first half); each extra-time half gets 1. The historic seasons and tournaments before 1970 (Italy 1947-48, World Cup 1934) give at most 1 and often none, as in those days. The added minutes always run at the pace of a 10-minute match, whatever match length you chose, so they can really be played: about 15 seconds of play for +3 and 26 for +5 (in a 3-minute match +3 would otherwise be gone in under 5 seconds). As always the clock stops when the ball is out of play, and when the added time is over the referee still lets a running attack finish. A goal in added time shows as 45+2 or 90+3 in the scorers' list. Not with SWOS++.

  • Both benches in view. When you call your bench, the camera stops at the halfway line, so you see your own bench and your opponent's together, top to bottom.
  • The cursor arrow follows you onto the front row (it points the other way there). A player you pick from the back row stands up in front of the front row, so nobody walks through his team-mates.
  • The match clock steps aside. While the bench is open, the clock moves to the top right, next to the spinning S, so it no longer covers the coach and the first seats. It goes back to its place when you close the bench.
  • Fixed a bug of the original game: when you called the bench with the ball in the top half of the pitch, the bench sometimes ignored the cursor keys and you couldn't pick anyone. The camera now settles properly and the bench always responds.
  • Training matches keep the original one-row bench and clock.
  • With 5 substitutes or fewer, the original dugout is back (thanks to Marco). The two-row dugouts appear only when the match has a long bench, more than 5 substitutes. In every other match, in every world (1996-97 and Europe 1984-85 included), you get the game's own dugout, as in the original.
  • Wider rows in the bench menu (layout by Marco). In a match, the substitutes list, FORMATION, the opponent's list and the long-bench pages have wider rows. The yellow / red card now sits in its own space left of the face, at the same distance from the edge of the menu as from the face. The injury cross has its own column before the position, so it no longer covers the end of a long name. (With SWOS++ the menu keeps SWOS++'s own look.)

CPU replacements when you buy a player (thanks to Nicola)

CPU replacements (World 2026-27). When you buy one of the first 16 players of a computer-controlled club, the club fills his place at once. Until now the game always invented a new player for it (a made-up name). A new button in TRANSFERS > BUY PLAYER, right of EXIT, lets you choose:

  • CPU REPL.: INVENTED - as before (the default);
  • CPU REPL.: FROM SQUAD - one of the club's own players from place 17 on takes the place instead: the most valuable one in the same role (goalkeeper, defender, midfielder, forward); if there is none, the most valuable outfield player. A goalkeeper is only ever replaced by a goalkeeper, and a goalkeeper never fills an outfield place. A player on the transfer list is not used. If nobody fits, the game invents one as before.

Buying a player from place 17 on never brings a replacement (as before). The choice is saved with the career; a new career starts with INVENTED. Older saves load with INVENTED.

An easier install (thanks to Roman)

  • Install into a new folder. The patcher page has a second button, "Install into a new folder (Program Files, CD)". First pick the game's folder: your GOG install, a copy on disk, or the CD. Then pick (or create) an empty folder of your own outside Program Files, for example Documents\SWOS 96-97 Mod. The page copies the game there and installs the mod in the copy. The original game is not touched. Play from the new folder: the GOG shortcut still starts the original game.
    • Why: Windows does not let a web page write inside Program Files or on a CD. This was the most common reason an install failed.
  • Clearer messages. When something goes wrong, the page now says what happened and what to do:
    • The wrong folder: which files it looks for (ITALIAN.EXE, ENGLISH.EXE, FRENCH.EXE or GERMAN.EXE, and the DATA folder with TEAM.020). It also looks inside subfolders.
    • A folder it cannot write to (Program Files, a CD): nothing was changed; use the second button.

Club finances closer to real football (thanks to Nicola)

Nicola noticed that in a career with a big club you end every season deep in the red, even winning everything, and have to sell players to stay afloat. That is how the original game works:

  • What the original charged. At every match the club pays its players' wages and the club's running costs. The game sets the running costs at the start of each season to about 3.2 times the wage bill, so a club really pays about 4.2 times its wages. A star squad has big wages, so it pays a lot.
  • What the original paid in. Gate receipts (based on your league position, not on the size of the club), the prize money at the end of the season, and now and then a small random sponsorship. None of these grows with the club: in our tests the gate per match of Manchester City and of Brentford was about the same.
  • The result. In the World 2026-27, where today's squads cost what they cost today, a big club lost 10 to 20 million a season (Real Madrid -16.9 M, Manchester City -18.5 M, Inter -10.9 M in the first season), while a small club's books stayed roughly level.

What 4.1 changes. With every match charge, your club now also receives its share of the season's TV and commercial income:

  • a base that depends on the division: 3.0 M a season in the top division, 1.2 M in the second, 0.5 M in the third, 0.2 M in the fourth and below (a division played in parallel groups, such as Serie C or Serie D, counts as one level);
  • plus 1.1 times your season's wage bill: a bigger club earns more from TV and sponsors.

The money arrives a little at a time, with each match charge, and shows on the SPONSORS + TV RIGHTS row of the finance screen (IT: SPONSOR E DIRITTI TV, FR: SPONSORS + DROITS TV, DE: SPONSOREN + TV-RECHTE). Expenses are exactly as in the original game (the fair-play fine, below, aside).

Why it is closer to real football:

  • In the big European leagues wages typically take well over half of a club's revenue (a general real-world figure, not something we measured): income that grows with the payroll (1.1 times the wages, on top of the gate and the prizes) puts a well-run club in that range.
  • TV and commercial income grow with the size of the club. Domestic TV money, by contrast, is shared fairly evenly within a division, and that is the base by division.
  • Clubs receive this money in instalments during the season, not in one lump. So you can't spend it all on the first day of the transfer market.

The stadium bug. The gate receipts of a single match were stored in a number that could not go above about 1.05 million. Above that, the count started again from zero, so a bigger crowd could pay much less than a smaller one. It could happen to a club with a cheap squad and a big crowd, because the game divides the gate by the squad's average price. Now a gate stops at its maximum instead.

Old careers: nothing is stored in the save. A career started with an older version gets the new income from its next match.

UEFA prize money (World 2026-27)

In the 2026-27 world, in the league-phase Champions League, Europa League and Conference League, your club now earns UEFA's real prize money, scaled down to the game's economy: 1/20 of the real amounts. It is paid when it is earned, match by match, and shows on CLUB STATUS as its own row, UEFA PRIZE MONEY. Every payment is rounded to the nearest thousand.

| Paid for | Champions League | Europa League | Conference League | |---|---|---|---| | Playing the league phase | 931,000 | 215,500 | 158,500 | | League-phase win / draw | 105,000 / 35,000 | 22,500 / 7,500 | 20,000 / 6,650 | | Knockout play-offs | 50,000 | 15,000 | 10,000 | | Round of 16 | 550,000 | 87,500 | 40,000 | | Quarter-finals | 625,000 | 125,000 | 65,000 | | Semi-finals | 750,000 | 210,000 | 125,000 | | Final | 925,000 | 350,000 | 200,000 | | Winning the cup (extra) | 325,000 | 300,000 | 150,000 |

On top of these come the money for your final place in the league-phase table (the top 8 and places 9 to 16 get a bonus) and UEFA's "value" share, paid with the participation money and based on your country's TV market and your club's coefficient. The game's old bonus for playing in Europe at the start of the season is not paid in the 2026-27 world while the prizes are on, so nothing is paid twice.

The switch. CLUB STATUS has a new button left of CONTINUE: UEFA PRIZES: ON / OFF (Italian PREMI UEFA: SI / NO, French PRIMES UEFA: OUI / NON, German UEFA-PRAEMIEN: AN / AUS). OFF stops the prizes from then on and brings back the game's old start-of-season bonus. The setting is kept in your save. Careers saved before 4.1 start with the prizes ON; in a cup already under way, prizes are paid from the next European match on and nothing is paid back for what the club had already reached.

What is simplified, compared with UEFA: UEFA does not publish its TV-market order, so the game puts England, Spain, Germany, Italy and France first, then the other countries in the world's coefficient order; the game uses its own 5-year club coefficient where UEFA uses a 10-year one; the qualifying rounds' solidarity payments and the payments to clubs that do not take part are not included.

Financial fair play (thanks to Marco)

Financial fair play (idea: Marco). In a career, your chairman now watches the wage bill. At the start of every season he compares the squad's wages at the end of the season with what the club earned in the season just ended (gate receipts, sponsors, prize money, the board's yearly money and the TV rights - but not the part of the TV money that grows with your own wage bill, or buying stars would raise your "income" too). If the wages are more than 40% of that income:

  • you cannot buy players until the wages are back under 40% (sell someone and the market opens again);
  • the club pays a fine: half of the wages over the limit;
  • the chairman tells you so in a memo, and the season-start statement shows the FINANCIAL FAIR PLAY FINE row.

Why only half? The real punishment is the closed market, which makes you sell. The fine is a warning, not meant to sink the club; a full fine would make you pay the excess wages twice (during the season and again at its end). Real football works the same way: UEFA's fines are a fraction of the overspending, the bite is the limit on the market and the squad.

What it means in practice (measured in the game, World 2026-27 Real Madrid, several seasons: the income depends on the season's results): their squad costs about 30% of their income; with one top star (Rodri) about 37% - still allowed; three big signings in one summer take them to 41-49%: the market closes for the season and the club pays a fine of 0.1-1.4 million, depending on how far over it is. So a big club can sign a star, but not keep piling them up; a club paying far more than it earns gets stopped.

It is an option: ON for a new career, OFF for a career saved with an older version; you can switch it at any time on the club statement (ANDAMENTO CLUB / CLUB BUSINESS: the box at the top right, UP from CONTINUE). The same row shows your wages as a share of last season's income, against the 40% limit. Nothing is checked in your first season at a club.

B-team call-ups (World 2026-27)

Call up your B team. In a World 2026-27 career, a club that has a B team (its second team or under-23 side) can now bring its young players up to the first team. That is 20 clubs: Juventus (Juventus Next Gen), Atalanta (Atalanta U23), Inter (Inter U23) and Milan (Milan Futuro) in Italy; Real Sociedad in Spain; Stuttgart and Hoffenheim in Germany; Porto, Benfica and Sporting in Portugal; PSV, Utrecht, AZ and Ajax in the Netherlands; Sydney FC, Western Sydney, Melbourne City and Brisbane Roar in Australia; Auckland FC and Wellington Phoenix in New Zealand.

On your squad screen a new B TEAM button, next to EXIT, opens the B team's squad (two pages: the whole squad, up to 30 players). Players you have already called up are greyed out. Pick one, then choose:

  • Next match only: he plays for you in the next match and goes back to the B team right after it. Players under 23 and goalkeepers can always be called; older players only up to the country's limit: 4 in Italy, Spain and Portugal, 3 in Germany, no limit in the Netherlands. In Australia and New Zealand the reserve teams are under-23 sides, so only players under 23 can be called, goalkeepers included. At most 4 players a match. He costs nothing and you pay him no wages.
  • For the season: he joins your squad until the end of the season and you pay his normal wages; the B team takes a youth player in his place. At most 3 in each transfer window, and only while the window is open: in Europe from 1 July to 1 September and in January; in Australia and New Zealand from 24 June to 15 September and from 12 January to 8 February.

There is never a transfer fee: calling a player up does not touch your bank balance. If a call-up is not allowed, the page tells you why (for example "already called up" or "transfer window closed"). On your squad screen a called-up player's shirt number shows a yellow number (it no longer carries a B), and his value reads LOAN: he cannot be sold or put on the transfer list. Everyone goes back to the B team at the end of the season, or when you take a job at another club. Your squad still holds at most 30 players. A player whose year of birth we do not know counts as under 23. For New Zealand we found no official rule for the reserves' ages, so they follow the Australian one: an approximation. With the new button in place, a long coach's name on the squad screen is shown shortened (the name itself is kept).

Choose your world (thanks to Francesco and Marco)

  • A WORLD selector on the main menu. A new button lets you pick the world you play in: 2026-27, 1996-97 or EUROPE 1984-85. It replaces the green CAREER 2026-27 button. The world you choose applies to CAREER, FRIENDLY, DIY competitions and the preset competitions: the teams, competitions and seasons you are offered are those of that world.
  • CAREER lists the teams of the chosen world. The team choice at the start of a career now shows the clubs of the world you picked, and nothing else: the purple HISTORIC box and the world entry are gone from that screen. You play in the world chosen from the main menu.
  • Clear messages when something is not available in a world. HISTORIC TOURNAMENTS and HISTORIC SEASONS are always visible. If the chosen world has none, the game says so instead of leaving an empty list: "NO PRESET COMPETITIONS FOR THIS WORLD AT THE MOMENT", "NO SEASONS FOR THIS WORLD AT THE MOMENT". National teams are not ready for 2026-27 yet and say "NATIONAL TEAMS NOT YET AVAILABLE FOR THIS WORLD"; in EUROPE 1984-85 the national teams are those of DaniSwos.
  • Friendlies and DIY competitions across worlds. You can put a club of a career world (2026-27 or Europe 1984-85) against clubs of the original 1996-97 game, for example INTER 2026-27 against HAMBURG 1996-97 (the one limit is described just below, "one world per competition").
  • Purple box to the historic tournaments and seasons. For the preset competitions and seasons of the 2026-27 and 1984-85 worlds, a purple box under the message leads to the historic tournaments and seasons (for example WORLD CUP 1934, ITALIA 1947-48).
  • National teams of Europe 1984-85 in DIY competitions. A DIY competition with the national teams of Europe 1984-85 saves and loads with their own squads, even after you have looked at the 1996-97 national teams in the same session. In Europe 1984-85 the nations are those of the time (WEST GERMANY, EAST GERMANY, SOVIET UNION, YUGOSLAVIA...).
  • One world per competition (friendlies and DIY competitions). In the club picker of FRIENDLY and of the three DIY editors (tournament, cup, league), the first club you pick from a mod world (World 2026-27 or Europe 1984-85) fixes the world of that competition. From then on the other mod world disappears from the lists (and so does the purple HISTORIC box if nothing is left in it), and on the lists of worlds the title bar reads "ONE WORLD PER COMPETITION". Nothing is refused. Clubs of the original 1996-97 game can always be mixed in, and the locked world's clubs stay pickable. To change world, unselect all the clubs you picked: the lists come back. National teams and custom teams are never locked, and neither is the CAREER team choice. A DIY competition saved with mod-world clubs remembers its world; older DIY files load as 1996-97.
  • Patcher: "Original main menu" (Marco's idea). A new tick box on the patcher page, off by default. Ticked, the main menu looks like the 1996-97 game's: no green WORLD button, no "MOD" label in the bottom-left corner, and CAREER back in the middle of its row. Other mod texts, like the credits on the title screen, stay. The default world becomes 1996-97, whatever you had chosen before; World 2026-27 and Europe 1984-85 stay one step away in the world list of each mode (FRIENDLY, DIY, the preset competitions and seasons). To get the mod's menu back, untick the box and run the patcher again: the world you had chosen comes back too. Your saves are not affected. It is an install-time choice, with no switch inside the game. Under SWOS++ its own main-menu entry stays where SWOS++ puts it.
  • Long benches in friendlies and DIY competitions (2026-27 world). In the World 2026-27 each club uses the bench of its own league (7 to 15 players, mostly 9 or 12, 15 in Serie A), with 5 substitutions everywhere (one more in extra time where that competition's rule has it). In a match between clubs with different benches, both use the LONGER one. The 1996-97 and Europe 1984-85 worlds, national teams and custom teams keep the game's 5 substitutes, and its own number of changes: 2 in a friendly, 3 in a league or career match, what you set in a DIY competition. On the screen before the match the list scrolls down to the extra players: you can swap them into the line-up or the bench, in EDIT TACTICS too, and the line-up you set there is the one that plays. PLAY MATCH judges the players you see on that screen. The extra players' places are kept for up to 4 clubs; play with a fifth and the first one's extra players go back to their places in the squad; the order of the 16 squad players always stays.

Bench info strip (an idea of Francesco)

More realistic offers for your players (thanks to Francesco)

  • A league title decided by a play-off (Malta type): the champion's European place now goes to the play-off winner, even if he finished below 9th in the table.

  • Friendly with both clubs picked from one list (World 2026-27, for example SERIE A: INTER, then JUVENTUS). The match got the plain bench of 5 and showed "MILAN (SCOTLAND)" next to a club. Fixed: the long bench applies and each club shows its own country.
  • DIY TOURNAMENT, national teams, EUROPE 1984-85. After a 2026-27 friendly it could show 2026-27 Saudi clubs. Fixed.

  • Preset competitions after a cross-world friendly. If you played a friendly between clubs of different worlds and then loaded a preset competition, its teams could show today's (2026-27) country names instead of their own. Fixed: every preset competition shows its own country names.

  • OPTIONS screen spacing (thanks to Marco). The option rows moved down 2 pixels, so the gap under the title now matches the gap above QUIT TO DOS. QUIT TO DOS, CONTROLS and EXIT stay where they were. Not with SWOS++, which has its own options screen.

  • Coppa Italia Serie C winner (World 2026-27, Serie C careers). If the winner of the Serie C cup ended the season relegated, or in a play-out place, it could also win the play-off place and go up and down at the same time, which broke the next season (Serie B with 21 clubs, its old group with 19). It now takes no play-off place, as the rule says (FIGC C.U. 209/A of 27 April 2026). A play-out place is the 3rd or 2nd from last of the group, and also the 5th or 4th from last when the two clubs of that tie are less than 8 points apart. In the real rule the place goes to the cup's other finalist, then to the fourth-placed club of the winner's group; the game does not keep the other finalist, so the club in 11th place in the winner's group takes it, as it already did for a winner placed 1st to 10th (a stand-in we declare).

  • Skin tones. In the 2026-27 world, JOHN YEBOAH and JUAN JESUS now have dark skin, and 45 players in 18 countries go back to the look they had before.
  • Club names without the "&" sign: O Y M, TREFELIN BGC, SB VLASIM and HARLAND WOLFF.
